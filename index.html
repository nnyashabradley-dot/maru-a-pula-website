Clouds of rain.
The promise of blessing.

Maru-a-Pula means exactly that in Setswana — and for over 50 years it's been the name behind one of Africa's leading independent schools, built on rigorous Cambridge academics and a deep commitment to community.

Our story

Founded on 50 acres given by the nation of Botswana.

In 1972, at the invitation of the Botswana Government, Maru-a-Pula was established as a non-profit trust — one of the first schools in southern Africa built on explicitly multiracial ideals, at a time when that was a radical act.

"Rain is a great blessing here — you wish someone well by saying 'Pula.' Maru-a-Pula, rainclouds, comes to mean the promise of blessing."

Today MaP is home to over 800 students and around 70 teachers from more than 35 nationalities, two-thirds of whom are Batswana — a school that is deeply international and deeply rooted at once.

The MaP difference

Our Distinctions

What sets MaP apart

A rigorous Cambridge education, built around service and character.

We prepare students for the IGCSE, AS and A-Level examinations — and for the harder work of becoming people who give back.

Cambridge Academics

Rigorous IGCSE, AS and A-Level preparation from Form 1 through Upper Six, taught by a faculty of about 70 educators.

Required Community Service

Every student and full-time teacher serves weekly — from village primary schools to Old Naledi and the SOS Children's Village.

Maitisong & the Arts

Home to Maitisong, Botswana's premier performing arts centre and host of the country's largest annual arts festival.

Day & Boarding

A 50-acre campus in the heart of Gaborone, welcoming day students and boarders living within a close-knit residential community.

Global Outcomes

Graduates go on to highly selective universities worldwide, with 94 alumni currently enrolled at the world's top 100 institutions.

Access & Financial Aid

Full scholarships for orphans and vulnerable children, including refugees, alongside need-based financial aid for eligible families.
