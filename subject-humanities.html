Subject spotlight

Humanities, rooted in Botswana.

See the Syllabus ↓ Meet the Department
A regional lens

Global syllabus, local grounding.

The Humanities & Social Sciences at MaP cover history, geography, economics, and business studies — following the Cambridge syllabus, but taught by staff who make sure Botswana's own history and regional context aren't treated as a footnote.

Students get a first taste of all four areas in Lower School before narrowing to whichever one or two they carry through IGCSE and, for some, A-Level.

What's covered, and when

The syllabus at each stage

Three stages, narrowing from four subjects to one.

Lower School · Form 1–3

A first taste of all four

History, geography, economics, and business studies are introduced together, giving every student a genuine sense of each before choosing what to specialise in.

IGCSE · Form 4–5

IGCSE Humanities subjects

Students narrow to the one or two subjects they've connected with most, sitting formal Cambridge examinations in Form 5.

A-Level · Lower & Upper Six

A-Level specialisation

For students headed toward humanities, law, or business degrees, one subject continues through to A-Level, with class sizes small enough for real discussion and debate.

Detailed syllabus content and Cambridge exam board specifics need to be confirmed with the Humanities Department before publishing.

Outside the syllabus

Beyond the classroom.

Students have taken part in regional quiz competitions and Model United Nations-style debating events, putting the subject's ideas into practice outside the classroom.

For students weighing law, politics, or international relations at university, these events are often the first real test of whether the subject is a genuine fit.

Specific clubs, competitions, and activities need to be confirmed with the Humanities Department before publishing.
